What I work on

Four things, and the proof for each

The common thread is not a stack. It's that a system should be able to tell you when it doesn't know, and that the claim should be testable by someone other than the person who wrote it.

Agentic systems with human control

Agents that propose and then stop. Policy-driven approval gates, tool allowlists, step budgets, and an append-only log of every model call, tool call and human decision. Autonomy is a dial you open on evidence, not a default you ship with.

Worked through in Acme Triage Agent

Retrieval you can audit

RAG that resolves every citation to a document, section and page, scores its own confidence, and declines rather than guesses. Chunking that never lets one chunk straddle two policies, and thresholds that were measured rather than picked.

Worked through in Acme Support Assistant

Evaluation and guardrail testing

Held-out sets written in real user language, calibration curves that turn a refusal rate into a business decision, and mutation testing that proves a guardrail is actually covered by deleting it and watching what fails. A passing suite is not evidence on its own.

Worked through in llmcheck

AI adoption for engineering teams

Spec, generate, review, test. Working agreements that say which data never goes into a prompt, review checklists aimed at how generated code actually fails, and a rollout that starts with a small pilot on low-risk work rather than a tool announcement.

Worked through in AI Dev Playbook
